I turn technology into revenue. AI is changing how.

Twenty years pricing and packaging technology at Dell and Cohesity. My work sits where three things meet: what the technology can do, how people actually decide, and the story a company runs on. AI is rewriting all three, and the path from them to dollars. I am studying how.

Selected work

Four pricing decisions, and what they taught me.

AI monetization · Enterprise data platform

Pricing an agent to learn, not to collect

The hardest part of pricing new technology is the quote-to-cash plumbing and the sales narrative, not the model.

Situation
An agent-accessible interface was about to launch. Every standard unit of measure, whether seats, capacity or queries, would have taxed the behaviour we wanted most: usage.
Decision
A flat platform fee, no meter for the first year, and the cost of every action shown to the customer at zero. A sliding scale that gets cheaper as agents multiply returns next fiscal year. I helped IT build the quote page and gave sales one narrative.
Result
We planned for about ten design partners and reached high double digits, then paused onboarding to keep the feedback usable. Revenue conversion is expected in about six months.
Deal governance · Enterprise data platform

Showing the cost of a promise

Good tools make judgment better by showing its consequences.

Situation
Large deals were priced on gut feel and on promises made between executives. Margins ran from loss-making to near full, and several prominent accounts sat in the red.
Decision
A deal-level margin calculator that flags each deal red, yellow or green before approval. It informs the approver’s judgment and does not replace it. Enablement shifted from how deep to discount to how to sell value.
Result
Deal-to-deal margin variability fell by roughly a quarter. On the most heavily discounted lines, gross margin rose 2 to 6 points, depending on region.
Packaging · Enterprise data platform

Pricing on the metric you win on

Choose the unit where your advantage shows, and where a rep can say the price in one sentence.

Situation
Each product was metered in its own unit. Reps explained a different rule for each one, and customers could not compare vendors like for like.
Decision
One unit across the portfolio: data actually stored after compression and deduplication, not the size of the source. Source size is the same at every vendor and hides our advantage. One sizing tool and one narrative for the field.
Result
Win rates rose on most deals. Reps gained two levers to trade, capacity and discount. Competitors have since moved to the same unit.
Packaging · Dell Technologies · ProDeploy

One story for a global deployment portfolio

Put the highest price on the customers most able to pay it, and keep the entry tier simple enough to say yes to.

Situation
Deployment services varied by country and customer type. Consolidating them meant one offer that met roughly 90 to 95 percent of demand across every industry.
Decision
A good-better-best structure. My SVP let me propose what I thought best, and sales warned it would only bring discount requests. I priced the entry tier for velocity, not margin, and the top tier at what our best customers would pay anyway.
Result
The noise from commercial and mid-market customers largely stopped, since the old à-la-carte options had been too complex for them. The top tier carried a higher price without losing the customers best suited to pay it.
The thinking

Judgment is the scarce input. The question is who pays to build it.

The Knowledge Quadrant sorts work along two axes: explicit versus tacit knowledge, and accuracy versus originality. AI cheapens the explicit side first. The harder question is where the next generation of leaders will learn judgment once the entry-level work disappears.

Where this argument may be wrong

AI is improving at judgment-shaped work, not only knowledge-shaped work. If that continues, the premium on human judgment could shrink before organisations learn to build it. I hold this thesis with that uncertainty, and I would rather be corrected than comfortable.

About

Judgment is granted, not taught.

At Dell, my SVP let me design the pricing for our deployment services, ProDeploy, however I thought best. I proposed a good-better-best structure. Sales told me it looked good on paper and would only produce discount requests. It took six months of escalations and roadshows to prove otherwise.

I believe judgment is granted, not taught: someone has to let you make a call that might fail. I now write about what happens to that arrangement when AI does the work that used to teach it.
